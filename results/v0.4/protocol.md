# Collection selection protocol

Compare 32 interchangeable emissions drawn from the same 256 randomly ordered candidates. Use the no-cell fingerprint, a green probability of 0.5, the exact deduplicated audit at 0.001 and a 100 meV/atom energy window. All rules see producer fingerprints and ranking energies only. Record downstream failures in the denominator. A batch with a different composition target or property request belongs to a separate pool.

**Design comparison.** On the two 512-record development cohorts from version 0.3, compare fixed batches of eight, a four-candidate start with adaptive reserve, and exchange across the entire pool. In the reserve rule, prioritize slots without an eligible green and break ties in rotation. When every slot has an eligible green, spend the next candidate on the slot with the highest selected energy. Recompute eligibility against each slot's final minimum. For pool exchange, bound each sorted selected energy by both the corresponding unmarked pooled order statistic plus the window and the corresponding fixed-capped output energy. Maximize green emissions under both bounds, then minimize total energy. Also evaluate the fixed and pooled unmarked minima, a predesignated single candidate, and unconstrained fixed green selection. The two unmarked budget-matched baselines quantify the benefit of pool access itself.

**Decision.** Compare 300 paired development trials, seed 44011. Prefer designs reaching at least 90% observed post-relaxation detection in each development generator and at most 10 meV/atom higher mean absolute ranking energy than fixed capped on each. Break ties by the smaller of the two detection rates, then by lower energy on both. Lock the choice before evaluating any new collection. In confirmation, report all arms, including the alternatives that missed the development goal.

**Confirmation.** Use 500 paired trials, seed 78341, on 384 fresh MatterGen IDs disjoint from both earlier physics cohorts and a separate CrystalFormer-MP20 source from PhononBench. Exclude external structures sharing the no-cell fingerprint of any earlier CrystalFormer producer to test transfer across classes as well as archives. Use a seeded random ordering of the external records and take the first 384 class-disjoint, parseable producer structures. Use the source's public dynamical stability labels as an independent quality outcome. Also evaluate 384 WyCryst records as a transfer population and the previous CrystalFormer confirmation cohort as a retrospective comparison. New MatterGen and WyCryst source samples use seed 47017. Repeat the prespecified comparisons on the available MatterGen bounded-strain measurements.

The primary outcome is collection detection after independent re-relaxation and standardization. The target is at least 90% observed detection on each primary generator at 32 emissions. Compare absolute mean producer ranking energy and the independent quality yield against fixed capped. Use a five-percentage-point tolerance for observed loss in independent quality yield. Report the difference even if the target is missed. Report source-specific ranking definitions, valid outputs, audit coverage, class diversity, false detections under an independent audit key, and conditional uncertainty. Different generator populations have different energy references. Repeated trials quantify variation in keys and candidate sampling within each source.

**Locked primary selector.** Pool exchange passed both development requirements. Its independent-relaxation counts were 300/300 for MatterGen and 296/300 for CrystalFormer. The reserve rule gave 300/300 on both but raised CrystalFormer mean producer energy by 22.3 meV/atom over fixed capped, exceeding the 10 meV/atom allowance. Pool exchange lowered mean energy on both. These development outputs and this choice were recorded before executing the confirmation audits.

## Follow-up after the first external test

The locked pool rule gave 158/500 detections on the external CrystalFormer cohort, compared with 8/500 for fixed capped. Retain this result. Its pool-wide ceiling constrained every rank against the globally best unmarked collection. In an exploratory 200-key comparison on those same structures, changing the green fraction under those ceilings gave at most 39% observed detection across the tested binary fractions. With the original fixed-slot ceilings and a collection mean budget, green fractions of 0.5 and 0.625 gave 87% and 92.5%. Exploratory continuous-score alternatives under the strict ceilings gave 11% and 45.5% for Fisher and uniform-sum audits. All of these comparisons are development evidence.

Freeze the following rule before evaluating a second cohort. Use green probability 0.625, 32 outputs, 256 candidates, and nominal level 0.001. Require each sorted output energy to be at most 100 meV/atom above the corresponding sorted minimum of the original eight-candidate batches. Require mean energy at most 100 meV/atom above the pooled unmarked minimum and at most the mean of fixed capped selection at the same green fraction. Maximize green count, then minimize energy. Pair the result with both fixed and pooled rules at both green fractions. This exchanges a per-rank bound against the pooled unmarked minimum for a mean bound against that baseline. The per-rank 100 meV bound against the fixed unmarked baseline remains in force.

Use fresh MatterGen IDs, excluding all three earlier physics cohorts, and a new PhononBench CrystalFormer cohort, excluding both earlier external IDs and every previous external or historical producer class. Use source seed 63593 and trial seed 91571 for both, 384 records and 500 trials. The same 90% observed primary detection target, 10 meV/atom mean producer quality allowance and five-percentage-point independent stable-yield allowance apply against the original fixed 0.5 baseline. Include strain, wrong-key controls and sensitivity to the most extreme producer score. These follow-up results assess a fixed rule independently of the cohort used to motivate it.
